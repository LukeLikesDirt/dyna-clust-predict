Where the F‑measure actually peaks

Both singleton constraints put the optimal cutoff near 0.95, well below the ~0.98 expected for real fungal ITS. This is the full per‑threshold curve behind that number — how sharp the peak is, and what moving up to 0.98 costs in F‑measure.

max 50% singleton species

reference: 0.980

Neither curve is a sharp spike sitting alone at 0.95 — both sit on a genuinely flat plateau from about 0.94 to 0.965 (F moves by only ~0.01–0.02 across that whole span), and only start declining more steeply above ~0.97. Moving the cutoff up to 0.980 costs −0.067 F under the 50%‑singleton constraint and −0.086 F under full singleton removal — real, but a fraction of the −0.31 to −0.41 F you'd lose pushing all the way back to cut‑off = 1.000. 0.98 sits on the shoulder of the plateau, not off in the degenerate tail.
